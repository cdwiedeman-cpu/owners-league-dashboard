Publishing the season dashboard

How a rebuild gets to the icon on your home screen — and why the numbers you type into the Race tab are still there afterwards.

The short answer

Your typed projections are stored by the browser you typed them into, against the web address. They are not in the HTML file and never were — that file is a program, not a document. Publishing replaces the page at the address; it never changes the address. So a rebuild cannot wipe your worksheet.

https://cdwiedeman-cpu.github.io/owners-league-dashboard/

The order of operations

Casey, 4 Sep 2026: "do I need to first put my projections in the HTML download file, then do the git publish to see those projections on my iphone? Or should I first do the git publish, then add my projection to that github.io publish?"

Publish first. Always.

Typing into the downloaded HTML file achieves nothing. That page cannot save at all, and even if it could, nothing carries what a file remembers up to the web. The Race tab says so in orange when you open it that way.

1

Publish — the one command below. This puts the page at the address.

2

Open the address and type your projections there — the home-screen icon on the phone, or Safari on the laptop. They save as you type.

3

Rebuild and republish as often as you like. Your numbers stay. The page reloads itself to the new version and reads the same stored worksheet.

The one thing a rebuild can change

Cells are filed under the team and the thing they are for, like Nebraska, 11 Oct at Minnesota or Nebraska, FBS Champ. A rebuild can change what is on the sheet. A game gets played, a team changes hands, or a scoring row is renamed or split. When that happens, the cell it was filed under no longer exists.

Those cells are kept, not deleted, and never counted, and the tab says how many there are. They come back if the row does. They are never quietly added to a total, because a total cannot include a cell you cannot see.

This applies to you right now. The scoring rows were rewritten on 4 September — the playoff ladders, the bowls and the conference rows are all new or renamed. Anything typed into a bonus row before that date will show up as an orphaned cell. Schedule cells are unaffected. Clear the grid and retype the bonuses, or retype just the ones you care about.

Your laptop and your phone keep separate worksheets

Browser storage does not travel and does not sync. Type on the laptop and the phone will not have it. To move one across, open Keep a copy, or move this to another device at the foot of the Race tab. Press Copy the worksheet and send yourself the text. On the other device, press Paste one in. That is also the backup to take before deleting the home-screen icon or resetting the phone.

Pick one device as the one you type into and treat the other as a reader. Two devices edited independently cannot be merged — pasting one in replaces the other.

Publishing a rebuild

Your job is one command

Not three. Everything else happens in the chat before you are ever told to push.

cd ~/Documents/Owners/owners-league-dashboard && git push origin main

Terminal on the Mac, or Termius on your phone — see Pushing from your phone. The && means "and then", so one paste does both halves. Lines ending in main -> main mean it is live; give GitHub Pages a minute, then open the icon.

When you ask for a rebuild in the chat, all of this is already done by the time you are told to push. The page is rendered. Every season suite is run, and a red one stops the build and tells you, rather than handing you a bad dashboard. The result is copied into deliverables_2026/. The hosted copy is staged and committed in the hosting folder. Committing needs no internet, so it can happen through the chat. Pushing does need the internet, and that route only exists from a shell you are signed in to. That is the entire reason the last step is yours.

The commands behind it, if you ever want them

You should not need these. They are here so the chain is written down somewhere, not because they are steps in your routine.

cd ~/Documents/Owners/Owners_League_Restart_Kit/code
python3 build_season.py

Renders the page, runs the suites, copies into deliverables_2026/, and stages and commits the hosted copy. That last part has been built into the build itself since 31 Aug 2026. It is why there is no separate staging step for you to remember.

cd ~/Documents/Owners/Owners_League_Restart_Kit/code
python3 publish_dashboard.py

Only the staging half, on its own. It exists for the case where a build finished somewhere that could not stage — it is a fallback, not part of a normal rebuild. Safe to run any time; No change since the last publish is a normal, correct answer.

If the commit fails on a stale lock

Claude cannot delete files on your Mac, so a leftover git lock has to be moved aside rather than removed:

cd ~/Documents/Owners/owners-league-dashboard
mkdir -p .git/_stale
mv .git/HEAD.lock .git/index.lock .git/_stale/ 2>/dev/null

Pushing from your phone

Set up once on 6 September 2026, so that being away from the Mac stops being the thing that keeps a finished rebuild off the web. The Mac stays at home and stays the only machine that holds the repository; the phone reaches it over your own private network, signs in, and runs the same one command.

1

Open Termius on the iPhone and tap the saved host for the Mac (the one whose address begins 100. — that is its Tailscale address, not its home Wi-Fi address).

2

You land at a prompt on the Mac. Paste the same command:

cd ~/Documents/Owners/owners-league-dashboard && git push origin main
3

Lines ending in main -> main mean it is live. Give Pages a minute, then open the home-screen icon.

What has to be true for the phone to reach the Mac

  • Tailscale is running on the Mac and signed in. It lives in the menu bar and starts at login. Quit it and the Mac disappears from your network — this is the one piece that must stay running.
  • The Mac is awake enough to answer. Wake for network access is on in Energy Saver; a Mac that is fully asleep and off power will not answer.
  • Remote Login is on (System Settings → General → Sharing).
  • Termius is on the phone. It is the client — the side that does the connecting. You do not need Termius running, or installed, on the Mac.

Nothing here touches the dashboard, the build, or your typed worksheet. It is only a way into the Mac's own command line.

Two macOS rules this depends on — both bit once, both are fixed

An SSH session is not allowed into your Documents folder by default. Every command run inside ~/Documents from the phone failed with Operation not permitted — not a git problem, a macOS privacy rule. The fix, already applied: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access, with /usr/libexec/sshd-keygen-wrapper switched on. If that ever gets turned off, or macOS is reinstalled, this is the first thing to check.

An SSH session cannot unlock your login keychain, so the saved GitHub password that works fine in Terminal is unreachable from the phone. It fails with failed to get: -25308 and then asks for a GitHub username that no password will satisfy. That is why this repository no longer authenticates with a saved password at all: it uses an SSH key, ~/.ssh/id_ed25519_github, registered on the cdwiedeman-cpu GitHub account, and the remote is in git@github.com: form. A key needs no keychain, which is why it works identically from both places.

If it ever asks you for a GitHub username again

That is not an expired login and you do not need a new token. It means the repository's remote got switched back to the https:// form, which is the form that needs the keychain. Press Ctrl+C and set it back:

cd ~/Documents/Owners/owners-league-dashboard
git remote set-url origin git@github.com:cdwiedeman-cpu/owners-league-dashboard.git

Confirm the key still works with ssh -T git@github.com — it should greet you by name and then say GitHub provides no shell access, which is the success message, not an error.

Putting it on the iPhone home screen

Do this once. After that the icon is the app, and every publish reaches it.

1

Open Safari and go to the address above. Not Chrome, and not the WorldWideWeb app. Only Safari can add a real home-screen app, and a home-screen app is the most durable place iOS will keep what you type.

2

Tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen, then Add.

3

Open it from the new icon from then on. Type your projections into the Race tab; they save as you type, and they are still there the next time you open it — after a publish, and after a restart.

How durable is "durable"

iOS clears stored data for ordinary websites you have not opened in about a week. A home-screen app is not treated as an ordinary website. WebKit's own storage policy says being added to the Home Screen is one of the signals it uses to grant a site persistent storage, and persistent storage is exempt from that clear-out. It is a heuristic, not a promise, and Apple does not publish the exact rule.

So: use the icon, open it during the season, and your worksheet stays. Take the text backup below before anything drastic. That is the honest version — anyone who tells you browser storage is guaranteed is guessing.

What actually loses a worksheet

  • Opening the HTML file directly (AirDrop, Files, the WorldWideWeb app on a local copy). No address, no storage. The tab tells you in orange.
  • Deleting the home-screen icon. iOS throws the app's storage away with it. Copy the worksheet out first — see below.
  • Clearing website data, or opening the page in a private tab.
  • Months without opening it. Storage that is never touched can eventually be reclaimed, home-screen app or not.
  • A different device or browser. Storage does not travel. Your laptop and your phone each keep their own worksheet.

None of these is a publish. Publishing is safe.

Moving a worksheet, or keeping a copy

At the bottom of the Race tab: Keep a copy, or move this to another device. Copy the worksheet gives you the whole thing as text — mail it to yourself, or paste it straight into Paste one in on the other device. That is also the backup to take before deleting the icon or resetting the phone.

It is text on purpose. A browser can drop what it has stored and give no warning, and a worksheet that exists in exactly one place you cannot see is one bad tap from gone.
