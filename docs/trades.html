Trades and Signing In

How the trade page applies the rules you have been applying by hand, what to set up once, and what you can still do yourself.

How It Works

Two ways a team changes hands, at two different times.

Owners enter their own trades in the RFL application. Everything in this table is a rule you already apply, and the application applies the same ones, in the same order, and shows its working. Nothing about the league changes.

There are two ways a team changes hands, and they happen at different times:

  • Through the Monday coin flips. During the week an owner builds a list of teams they want. At 4:00pm AZ Monday the RFL application works through every list that was submitted and settles them, including running a coin flip wherever more than one owner asked for the same team. Everybody can see what happened to each of their requests on their own page.
  • Through first come, first served. From Monday 4:00pm AZ until Tuesday 5:00pm AZ, any team still unowned can be taken outright by whoever asks first. This does not go through the Monday process at all — it is immediate, and there is no coin flip.

You are no longer the switchboard for either one, but you can still enter or correct anything by hand, for anybody, at any time.

The Rules It Applies

Your rules, enforced by the RFL application instead of by you.

These are the rules you have been applying by hand. Every one of them is enforced by the RFL application before a trade can go through, and an owner who breaks one is told which rule stopped them.

Three divisions,
separate pools		 Predator, Viper and Scorpion each draft and trade from their own pool. A team owned in one division can be free in another, and the three never interact.
Hold before dropping An owner has to hold a team for three of its games before it can be dropped.
  • For a team they drafted, every game that team has played this season counts toward those three games, including any played on or before draft day.
  • For a team they picked up during the season, the three games start on the day they got it. Games the team played for a previous owner do not count.
  • If an owner drops a team and picks it up again later, they have to hold it for three more games before they can drop it again.
Games left to claim The team being picked up needs three regular season games still to play. This is a different rule from the hold rule. The hold rule counts games a team has already played. This one counts games the team still has left.
Games left to drop The team being dropped needs one regular season game still to play.
Games already played
do not matter to a claim		 How many games a team has already played has nothing to do with whether it can be picked up. In the second week of September you can claim a team that has played twice, even though you could not yet drop one of your own that has played twice.
A team whose regular
season is over		 It cannot be traded away at all. It stays on the roster for the rest of the year, however long it has been held.
Which games count Only games on a regular season schedule. Bowl games, playoff games and conference championships count toward none of these numbers.
Frozen after a drop A team that has just been dropped must play one game before anybody can pick it up. So a team dropped on Monday afternoon is not available again that afternoon.
Like for like College teams are traded for college teams, NFL teams for NFL teams. That is what keeps every roster at eight college and three NFL. FBS and FCS both count as college.
Coin flip When two or more owners in the same division ask for the same team, it goes to a coin flip between all of them.
Cost Every completed trade costs the owner $3, whichever way it happened. The page keeps the running total for each owner.

The rest of this guide covers what is already set up and what is left, what happens each week, and what to do when an owner’s situation changes.

Setting It Up

What is done, and what is left.

All of the machinery is built and running. What is left is one job, and it is not a technical one: giving each owner their sign-in code.

What Is Already Working

  • The league has its own back end. It holds the 27 seats, the people on them, the rosters and every trade. It belongs to the league rather than to any person's private account.
  • All 27 seats are loaded, spelled the way the league spells them. Six of those seats have two owners on them, and each of those people has their own sign-in.
  • Every person has a code. Thirty-three codes in all.
  • Casey and Bob are both commissioners. Either of you can hand out a code, change somebody's role, or turn a lost phone away.
  • Signing in has been tested from end to end, including the part that matters most: two people on one seat get different codes, and only one of them is a commissioner.
  • The trades page is inside the live dashboard rather than in a practice copy. Settled trades move the real rosters.
  • The commissioner tabs appear for the two of you and for nobody else.

There Is No Spreadsheet and No Password to Share

This used to run on a Google account that the two of you signed in to with the same password. Google disabled that account without warning, and everything behind the trades page went with it in one afternoon. Nothing about the league had changed. The account simply stopped existing.

So it does not work that way any more. There is no shared login, no spreadsheet to keep in step, and no password written down anywhere. Your sign-in code is your own, Bob's is his, and the back end works out who is asking from the code rather than from anything the page says.

That also means the league no longer sends email. Nothing arrives in anybody's inbox and nobody waits on one.

What a Code Is

Six characters, letters and numbers. Something like X2FV4J.

It does not expire, and using it does not use it up. The same six characters sign that person in on their phone and on their computer. They type it once on each device and that device stays signed in.

There is no letter O and no number 1 in any code, because these get read out over the phone. A character that looks like one of those is the other.

Treat a code like a password. Whoever holds it can enter trades for that seat. Read it to the owner directly. Never put one where the league can see it, and never send one to an address that reaches more than one person.

What Is Left to Do

  1. Open the Access List tab. Every person is on it with their code beside their name.
  2. Give each owner theirs. A phone call, a text to them alone, or in person.
  3. Tell them to open the dashboard, go to My Trades, and type it in.

You do not have to do all 27 at once, and nothing breaks if somebody never signs in. An owner without a code simply tells you their trade and you enter it for them, exactly as before.

How Owners Sign In

No passwords, and no way to submit a trade as somebody else.

An owner signs in once on each device they use. After that the device stays signed in and they never think about it again.

The Three Steps

  1. You read them their six character code.
  2. They open the dashboard and go to the My Trades tab.
  3. They type the code into the box and press Sign Me In.

That is the whole of it. Nothing is emailed and there is nothing to wait for.

What Happens the Moment They Are In

The page learns whose seat it is, and so does the rest of the dashboard. Their own teams, their own roster and their own trades are what they see from then on. It is not only the trades tab that changes.

The seat is worked out fresh on every request, from what that device is holding. Nothing the page sends says who the person is, so nothing it sends can claim to be somebody else. That is what stops one owner entering a trade under another owner's name.

Two People on One Seat

Six seats in this league have two owners. They each get their own code and they are listed separately on the Access List.

They share everything the seat has. The same roster, the same teams, and either of them can enter that seat's trades. A trade is recorded against the team, not against whichever of them typed it.

Their roles are separate. One of them can be a commissioner while the other is not, and turning one of their sign-ins off leaves the other one signed in.

When a Code Is Not Accepted

The page says so and does not explain why. That is deliberate. Suppose it told the difference between a code that never existed and a code on a seat that has been switched off. Somebody could then use the sign-in box to work out who is in the league.

So the answer is always the same, and the answer to the owner is always the same too: check it with the commissioner. Read the code back to them. If it still will not take, press Retire and Issue a New Code on their row and read them the new one.

Trying again does not help, and there is nothing for the owner to fix at their end.

How an Owner Submits a Trade

Their week, in order. You do nothing in any of it.

  1. Tuesday 5:01pm AZ to Monday 3:59pm AZ. Owners build their list for the week. Each entry names a team to pick up and up to four teams they would be willing to drop for it. When it is ready they press Send my list, and the application tells them they are entered. Until they press it nothing is entered, and it says so in plain words at the top. They can change and re-send as often as they like right up to 3:59pm AZ.
  2. Monday 4:00pm AZ to Tuesday 5:00pm AZ. First come, first served. This is separate from the Monday process and nothing carries over into it. Any team still unowned in a division can be taken outright — one team, one drop, no coin flip, no waiting. Whoever gets there first has it.

That is the whole of an owner's week. Everything between those two windows happens without anybody pressing anything, and the next section is what it does.

What Happens on Monday, by Itself

The part that replaces you being the switchboard.

It settles itself. Nobody has to be there, no computer has to be switched on, and nobody can stop it half way.

Or you do it yourself, if you would rather. The Run It tab has a switch with two settings, Automatically and I Do It Myself. On the second one, Monday afternoon comes and goes and nothing happens until one of you presses the button.

Either way the cut-off is the same, and this is the part that matters for fairness. It does not depend on when anybody presses anything. Every entry is stamped with the moment it was sent. Anything sent at or after 4:00pm is too late, whether the week is settled at 4:01 or at nine that evening.

So running a week late costs nobody anything, and running it again gives the same answer. The coin flip is built from the week, the division, the team and the round. It never looks at the clock.

  1. Monday 4:00pm AZ. Entry closes. Anything sent at 4:00pm or later is too late, and is stamped with the time it was sent, so the cut-off does not depend on when the script happens to run.
  2. 4:00pm, or when you press Run It. Every list that was sent is settled, division by division, in the order below.
  3. Straight away. Every owner can see what happened to each of their entries on their own page. The results tab also writes the message for you, owner by owner, if you would rather send it to them yourself.
  4. Then first come, first served opens, and runs until Tuesday 5:00pm AZ.
  5. Tuesday 5:01pm AZ. Entry opens for the following Monday, already showing the new rosters.

Automatic and By Hand Give the Same Answer

They are not two programs. The button and the schedule end in exactly the same code, reading exactly the same list of entries. A test settles the same week both ways and compares every result, and it runs before any of this is allowed to ship.

That is worth saying plainly because an automatic Monday that quietly decided a team differently from the button would be far worse than no automatic Monday at all. It would be wrong for weeks before anybody noticed.

What the Coin Flip Actually Is

It is a real randomizer. Nobody can predict it or steer it, and everybody in the flip has the same chance.

The difference from a dice roll is where the randomness comes from. A dice roll uses the instant somebody pressed the button. This uses the week, the division, the team and the round, and those four things never change. So the same coin flip always comes out the same way, on any device and on any day.

That means you can always press run again and show an owner the identical answer. Undoing a week and running it again is safe for the same reason. Only the teams you changed something about come out differently. Every other team goes to exactly the owner it went to the first time.

How Monday Settles a Contested Team

Each division is settled separately, from its own pool. A team owned in Viper can be free in Predator.

  1. Everybody's first entry is settled before anybody's second, so being early is worth nothing.
  2. If one owner asked for a team, they get it.
  3. If two or more asked, it goes to a coin flip between all of them. Three or four owners on one team is perfectly normal.
  4. If you beat somebody in an earlier coin flip that afternoon and they are in this one too, you sit this one out and they go for it without you. Once they have picked up a team, you are level again and the next one is a normal coin flip.
  5. Only one team is dropped per request. Working down that owner's drop list, the first team they still hold and are allowed to drop is the one that goes. Allowed means three things: still on the roster, past its three-game hold, and the same kind of team as the one coming in. If none of them qualify, the request does not go through, and the owner is told which rule stopped it.

What Only You Can Do

Three actions no other owner has. Two of them are ordinary; the third is not.

Entering a Trade for Somebody Else

This is the one form for both jobs. A request an owner sent you, and a trade the two of you agreed over the phone, are the same thing to the software: somebody wants a team and is giving one up. There is no separate place to record a trade that happened elsewhere.

  1. Sign in as yourself.
  2. On the My Trades tab, choose their name from the Submitting for dropdown.
  3. Fill the form in exactly as they would have, and send it.

The clock applies to you the same as to them. Before Monday 4:00pm AZ it goes into the coin flips. After that it is a first-come claim. It costs that owner the usual $3, the record shows your name as the person who entered it, and the owner sees it on their own page.

You will use this a lot in the first few weeks, because that is the whole of stage one: the owners email you and you type it in.

What the Page Stops Before It Happens

An owner cannot enter a trade the rules would refuse. Every team you might pick up and every team you might drop is listed, and the ones you are not allowed to choose cannot be clicked. The reason sits beside each one, in the same words the settle would use.

  • A team with fewer than three regular season games left says so, and cannot be picked up.
  • A team still inside its three-game hold says how many games it has played and the date it becomes droppable.
  • A team with no regular season games left cannot be traded away.
  • Once a team to pick up is chosen, the drop list narrows to the same kind. A college team cannot be dropped for an NFL team, and the mismatch says why.

The count above the list says how many you can choose and how many are shown but not allowed, so nobody has to wonder whether a team is missing or blocked.

The list is checked again when it is sent. Picking is not the only way a choice goes wrong. Change the team you are picking up from a college team to an NFL team and the drops underneath it no longer match. Leave a list sitting from Tuesday to Monday and a hold that had two games left has none. Send My List refuses in both cases and names the row, the team and the reason, so nothing is entered that Monday would only turn down.

Monday checks all of it a third time before settling. That is deliberate: a roster can change between Tuesday and Monday, and the last word has to belong to the settle.

What Each Owner Owes

The Transactions tab keeps the running total. Every trade costs $3 and all of it goes into the Champions Pot.

The amount is recorded on each line rather than worked out from the number of lines. That matters for a reason worth knowing: putting one trade right can take two or three roster moves, and nobody should be charged three times for one mistake.

You can change what any single trade costs. Open the Rosters tab, find the trade in What Has Moved, and tap the amount in the Cost column. There are three buttons and a box:

  • Standard $3 puts it back to the ordinary fee.
  • No Charge sets it to nothing, for the second or third move of one correction.
  • Credit −$3 hands the money back, for something charged in error.
  • The box takes any other amount, including a negative one.

Anything other than $3 needs a reason, and the page will not save it without one. The reason is printed beside the amount on the Transactions tab, marked so it is obvious the figure was changed. A number that does not match the number of trades and does not explain itself reads as a mistake, which is worse than either the wrong figure or no figure.

Only you and Bob see the Cost column. An owner reading the same tab sees the trades and not the money.

Settling Monday Yourself

There is a switch on the same page with two positions, and it stays there permanently.

  • Automatic — the schedule settles the week a few minutes after 4:00pm AZ every Monday and you do nothing. This is how it runs once the whole league is on it.
  • By hand — the schedule settles nothing and waits for you to press Run the coin flips. This is how the first few weeks run, and it is there afterwards for any week you want to hold back.

The button is there in both positions. Under automatic you would only use it to settle a week early, or to catch somebody who was missed.

If You Missed Somebody

You run it at 4:05 and then open an email that had been sitting unread since 3:50. It happens.

Press Run the Coin Flips again. The page will tell you the week has already been settled and offer you two buttons, Undo It and Start Again and Settle Only What Has Come In Since. Take the first one. Then add the request you missed and run the week again.

Undoing a week takes the transaction fees back with it. Every trade records its own $3 on its own line, and the running total on the Transactions tab is the sum of those lines. Undo removes the lines, so the money goes with them. Nobody is charged for a trade that was undone, and there is no separate total to correct by hand.

Undo puts every roster back to how it stood at 4:00pm AZ and keeps every list exactly as it was typed — nobody re-enters anything. Then the whole week is settled from scratch with everything in it.

That is the only way to get the right answer, and it is worth understanding why. A late request does not just add a trade. It can join a coin flip another owner won. That changes who won that team, and that changes who has to stand aside for the next one. Settling the latecomer on top of the earlier result cannot reach the week the league should have had.

Two things to know about undo.

  • Somebody may lose a team they were already told they had won. Running the week again can legitimately hand it to a different owner, and that is the correct answer, not a second roll of the dice. You are entitled to do this and you should. Tell the owner what happened plainly. A request that was in on time had been missed. The week was settled again with everything in it. This is the result the week should have had the first time. It is easier to say before they have been told than after, so undo first and send the results once.
  • First come claims made since are undone too, and handed back to you. They were granted against rosters that are about to be settled differently, so they cannot simply be left standing. The confirmation lists them before anything happens, and after you re-run the week each one waits on the Run tab with a Try this one again button. If the team went to somebody else in the new flips, that claim is refused with the reason — which you can pass on, because it was a good claim when they made it.

The second button is Settle Only What Has Come In Since. It leaves every team already decided exactly where it is and settles nothing but the new request. Use it in one case only: the team the missed owner asked for went to nobody, so there is no coin flip to redo. In every other case, take Undo It and Start Again.

If you are not sure which case you are in, undo. It always reaches the right answer. The other one is faster and only sometimes correct.

Sending the Results Yourself

A second switch, next to the first one, decides whether the tool emails owners their results.

  • On — the message for each owner is written out for you the moment a week is settled.
  • Off — nothing is written out, and the results appear on screen for you to send instead. Copy every owner’s email gives you all of them in one block, each with the address it goes to, ready to paste.

Off is how the first few weeks run, so nothing reaches the league until you have looked at it. Turning it on is part of stage three.

Sign-in links are not affected by this switch and cannot be. They are how anybody gets into the page at all, including you. Turning results emails off never stops somebody signing in.

If Somebody Argues About a Coin Flip

You can re-run it and get the identical result. The flip is worked out from the week, the division and the team rather than being random, so the same week always comes out the same way. Show them twice and the argument is over.

What to Do When Something Changes

Five situations, each one a small edit.

All of it is done on the Access List tab, by either of you, and every change takes effect on that person's next request. Nothing is rebuilt, nobody has to sign in again, and nobody else has to be involved.

There is no spreadsheet behind this any more. The page is the place.

What Each Row Shows

  • Seat — the team, as the league names it. A seat with two owners prints twice, with the name written once, so you can see the pairing.
  • Person — their email address. It is how you know whose row this is.
  • Code — their six characters. This is what you read out to them.
  • Role — owner or commissioner.
  • Signed In — how many devices that person is signed in on right now.

An Owner Gets a New Email Address

  1. Press Change Address on their row and type the new one.

That is all. Their phone stays signed in and their code does not change. The two are kept separate on purpose, so fixing a typo never costs somebody their sign-in.

Somebody Loses Their Phone, or a Code Gets Seen

  1. Press Retire and Issue a New Code on their row.
  2. Read them the new code, which appears on screen straight away.

Every device that person was signed in on is turned away at once. Anybody else on the same seat stays signed in, because it is their sign-in you retired and not the team's.

A Team Gets a Second Owner

  1. Press Add a Second Owner under that seat and enter their email address.
  2. Read them the code it gives you.

They get their own code and their own role. Both of them see the same roster and either can enter that team's trades. A trade is recorded against the team, not against whichever of them sent it.

An Owner Leaves and Somebody New Takes the Team

  1. Press Change Address and put the new owner's address in.
  2. Press Retire and Issue a New Code on that row.
  3. Read the new owner their code.

Both steps matter and the second is the one people forget. Changing the address alone does nothing to the phone the old owner is already signed in on. Retiring is what signs that phone out.

The seat keeps its roster, its points and its history. A team's season carries on whoever holds it.

An Owner Leaves and Nobody Takes the Team

  1. Press Switch This Seat Off under that seat.

Nobody on it can sign in or send a trade. Everything else stays as it was: the team is still in the standings and still scores every week. Press Put This Seat Back when somebody takes it over.

Making Somebody a Commissioner

  1. Change the Role on their row from owner to commissioner.

They see this page, can settle a week, and can enter a trade for any seat, from their next request onwards.

The league will not let you stand down the last commissioner. There is no sequence of changes here that locks the two of you out.

What You Cannot Do Here

You cannot add or remove a seat. The 27 teams are the league's, they are fixed for the season, and a page where a seat could be deleted by accident is a page that can lose a roster. If the league itself changes, that is a rebuild rather than an edit.

Rolling It Out

Three stages. Owners are not let in until it has been run for real for a few weeks.

Twenty-seven seats and thirty-three people. You do not have to do them all at once, and nothing breaks while somebody has not signed in yet.

Start With the Two of You

Sign in yourself first, then get Bob signed in, before anybody else is involved. If something is wrong you want to find it with one person watching, not twenty-seven.

Check three things once you are both in. You can each see the commissioner tabs. You can each open the Access List and read the codes. An owner's seat name shows at the top of the page and it is the right one.

Then a Handful, Then the Rest

Pick three or four owners you can ring easily and do those next. If they get in without needing you to explain anything, the wording on the page is doing its job and the rest can follow.

Send the rest in your own time. There is no deadline and no batch to get right.

What to Say

Keep it to four lines. Anything longer gets skimmed.

  • Open the league dashboard.
  • Go to the My Trades tab.
  • Type in this code: their six characters.
  • That is it. You will not have to do it again on that phone.

Give the code to that person and to nobody else. Not in a group message, not in a reply-all, and not anywhere the league can read it. The code is the sign-in.

Owners Who Never Sign In

Some will not, and that is fine. They tell you their trade and you enter it for them, which is what happened before any of this existed. Nothing about the league depends on all 27 of them using the page.

When Somebody Cannot Get In

Read the code back to them slowly. Nearly every failure is a character misheard, and the two worth checking are the ones that look alike on a screen. No code contains the letter O or the number 1, so what they are reading as one of those is the other.

If it still will not take, open the Access List, press Retire and Issue a New Code on their row, and read them the new one.

Every entry comes back with one of these 13 answers, so nobody is left guessing whether they lost or the page broke.
