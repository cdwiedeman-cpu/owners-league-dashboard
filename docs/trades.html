Trades and signing in

How the trade page applies the rules you have been applying by hand, what to set up once, and what you can still do yourself.

How it works

Two ways a team changes hands, at two different times.

Owners enter their own trades in the RFL application. Everything below is a rule you already apply, and the application applies the same ones, in the same order, and shows its working. Nothing about the league changes.

There are two ways a team changes hands, and they happen at different times:

  • Through the Monday coin flips. During the week an owner builds a list of teams they want. At 4:00pm AZ Monday the page works through every list that was submitted and settles them, including running a coin flip wherever more than one owner asked for the same team. Everybody is emailed what happened to each of their requests.
  • Through first come, first served. From Monday 4:00pm AZ until Tuesday 5:00pm AZ, any team still unowned can be taken outright by whoever asks first. This does not go through the Monday process at all — it is immediate, and there is no coin flip.

You are no longer the switchboard for either one, but you can still enter or correct anything by hand, for anybody, at any time.

The rules it applies

Your rules, enforced by the RFL application instead of by you.

These are the rules you have been applying by hand. Every one of them is enforced by the RFL application before a trade can go through, and an owner who breaks one is told which rule stopped them.

Three divisions,
separate pools		 Predator, Viper and Scorpion each draft and trade from their own pool. A team owned in one division can be free in another, and the three never interact.
Hold before dropping An owner has to hold a team for three of its games before it can be dropped.
  • For a team they drafted, every game that team has played this season counts toward those three games, including any played on or before draft day.
  • For a team they picked up during the season, the three games start on the day they got it. Games the team played for a previous owner do not count.
  • If an owner drops a team and picks it up again later, they have to hold it for three more games before they can drop it again.
Games left to claim The team being picked up needs three regular season games still to play. This is a different rule from the one above, counted at the other end of the schedule.
Games left to drop The team being dropped needs one regular season game still to play.
Games already played
do not matter to a claim		 How many games a team has already played has nothing to do with whether it can be picked up. In the second week of September you can claim a team that has played twice, even though you could not yet drop one of your own that has played twice.
A team whose regular
season is over		 It cannot be traded away at all. It stays on the roster for the rest of the year, however long it has been held.
Which games count Only games on a regular season schedule. Bowl games, playoff games and conference championships count toward none of these numbers.
Frozen after a drop A team that has just been dropped must play one game before anybody can pick it up. So a team dropped on Monday afternoon is not available again that afternoon.
Like for like College teams are traded for college teams, NFL teams for NFL teams. That is what keeps every roster at eight college and three NFL. FBS and FCS both count as college.
Coin flip When two or more owners in the same division ask for the same team, it goes to a coin flip between all of them.
Cost Every completed trade costs the owner $3, whichever way it happened. The page keeps the running total for each owner.

The rest of this guide covers the six steps to set it up, what happens each week, and what to do when an owner’s situation changes.

Setting it up

Six steps, once, before the season.

This is done once, before the season, and it takes about half an hour.

First, a Google account for the league

Three things sit behind the page: the spreadsheet, the script that runs on Mondays, and the emails owners receive. All three live in one Google account. Casey and Bob agreed on a new account for the league rather than either of their personal ones, for three reasons:

  • Owners get league email from a league address, not from somebody’s personal Gmail.
  • Both of you have the login, so nothing stops working if one of you steps back for a season.
  • Neither of you ends up with the league’s machinery sitting in your own mail.

It is free and takes five minutes. Something like rflcommish@gmail.com. Do the rest of these steps while signed in to that account.

Then the six steps

  1. Make a new Google Sheet and name it RFL Trades.
  2. Add a tab named Access.
  3. Open the file Owners_League_Access_List.csv that came with this guide and paste it into that tab. All 27 seats are already listed and spelled correctly.
  4. Type each owner’s email address into the Email column. Personal inboxes only — not the league email list, and not an address that forwards to several people.
  5. Find Bob’s row and change Role from owner to commissioner. This is what gives him the two abilities described later, and it has nothing to do with who owns the Google account.
  6. In the Sheet, click Extensions, then Apps Script. Paste in the script Casey provides, click Deploy, and add the trigger (see below).

Casey does step 6. It needs the account password rather than just edit access to the Sheet, and it is the part he can work through with Claude.

Four of them you type. Two the script writes, and you leave those alone.

There is deliberately no “date the keys were retired” column. The script keeps that date where nobody can type over it, and the Log tab is where you read the history. A column Bob looks at before acting should say what is true now; what happened in August belongs in a log.

What the trigger is

A trigger is a schedule Google keeps on its own servers. It runs a piece of the script without anybody opening anything — no computer has to be switched on, and nobody has to remember. That is what makes Monday afternoon happen by itself.

The obvious setup would be “run this weekly, Mondays, at 4:00pm AZ”, and it is the wrong one. Google’s weekly schedules are only accurate to the hour — asking for 4:00pm AZ means somewhere between 4:00 and 5:00, and which minute is up to them. For a deadline that owners are typing against, that is not good enough.

So the trigger runs every five minutes, and the script itself decides whether there is anything to do. Almost every run does nothing at all and finishes instantly. The one just after 4:00pm AZ on a Monday settles the week.

The deadline itself does not depend on any of that. Every request is stamped with the time it was sent, and anything stamped 4:00pm AZ Monday or later is too late — whether the script happens to run at 4:01 or 4:04. Results are posted within about five minutes, and first come, first served opens the moment they are.

After that

The only thing anybody edits by hand is the Access tab, and Bob can do that with ordinary edit access to the Sheet — no password needed. Three other tabs appear on their own. Requests is filled in by the RFL application when owners submit. Results is written every Monday and becomes the record of every trade. Log keeps a dated line for every address change and every retirement. Leave all three alone. Log is there to be read, not edited.

How owners sign in

No passwords, and no way to submit a trade as somebody else.

Owners never get a password. There is nothing for them to remember and nothing for you to reset.

  1. The owner opens the page and types their email address.
  2. A link arrives in their inbox. It works once and expires after 30 minutes.
  3. They tap it, and they are signed in.
  4. That device stays signed in for the season. They do this once on their phone and once on their laptop, and that is the last they think about it.

Why an owner cannot enter trades as somebody else

When they tap that link, the page stores a long random code in their browser. They never see it or type it. Every trade they submit carries that code, and the league looks it up to find out whose seat it is. Nothing announces a name, so there is no name for anybody to change.

Knowing another owner's email address gets somebody nowhere. Ask for a link at that address and the link goes to that owner's inbox. That owner also gets an email saying somebody tried to sign in as them.

If somebody cannot sign in

The sign-in box says the same thing to everybody, whatever happened underneath — otherwise anybody could use it to find out who is in the league by typing addresses.

What it does say, to everyone, is: if nothing arrives in a few minutes, your address may not be the one the league has for you, so contact the commissioner. That reaches the person sitting there and gives nothing away.

An address the league has used before is treated differently. A former owner, or somebody whose address was changed, gets a real email saying the address is no longer on a seat and to contact you. That is safe, because an email lands with whoever owns the address, never with whoever typed it. An address the league has never used gets nothing at all, so nobody can use the sign-in box to send mail to strangers.

Two things it does not do

  • It does not stop anybody reading the page. The dashboard is a public web page and always has been. What is protected is submitting a trade.
  • It does not help if somebody can open another owner's inbox. Forwarding a sign-in email hands over your seat, the same as giving out a password.

Two owners on one team

Casey and Dominic share a seat, so they get two rows on the Access tab with the same seat name and different email addresses. Both sign in on their own phones, both see the same thing, and neither has to sign out for the other.

How an owner submits a trade

Their week, in order. You do nothing in any of it.

  1. Tuesday 5:01pm AZ to Monday 3:59pm AZ. Owners build their list for the week. Each entry names a team to pick up and up to four teams they would be willing to drop for it. When it is ready they press Send my list, and the page tells them they are entered. Until they press it nothing is entered, and the page says so in plain words at the top. They can change and re-send as often as they like right up to 3:59pm AZ.
  2. Monday 4:00pm AZ to Tuesday 5:00pm AZ. First come, first served. This is separate from the Monday process and nothing carries over into it. Any team still unowned in a division can be taken outright — one team, one drop, no coin flip, no waiting. Whoever gets there first has it.

That is the whole of an owner's week. Everything between those two windows happens without anybody pressing anything, and the next section is what it does.

What happens on Monday, by itself

The part that replaces you being the switchboard.

Nobody starts this and nobody can stop it half way. It runs on a schedule Google keeps, so no computer has to be switched on.

  1. Monday 4:00pm AZ. Entry closes. Anything sent at 4:00pm or later is too late, and is stamped with the time it was sent, so the cut-off does not depend on when the script happens to run.
  2. The RFL application settles every list that was submitted, division by division, in the order below. Nobody presses anything.
  3. Within about five minutes. Every owner gets an email telling them what happened to each of their entries. The results are on the page as well.
  4. Then first come, first served opens, and runs until Tuesday 5:00pm AZ.
  5. Tuesday 5:01pm AZ. Entry opens for the following Monday, already showing the new rosters.

What the coin flip actually is

It is a real randomizer. Nobody can predict it or steer it, and everybody in the flip has the same chance.

The difference from a dice roll is where the randomness comes from. A dice roll uses the instant somebody pressed the button. This uses the week, the division, the team and the round, and those four things never change. So the same coin flip always comes out the same way, on any device and on any day.

That means you can always press run again and show an owner the identical answer. Undoing a week and running it again is safe for the same reason. Only the teams you changed something about come out differently. Every other team goes to exactly the owner it went to the first time.

How Monday settles a contested team

Each division is settled separately, from its own pool. A team owned in Viper can be free in Predator.

  1. Everybody's first entry is settled before anybody's second, so being early is worth nothing.
  2. If one owner asked for a team, they get it.
  3. If two or more asked, it goes to a coin flip between all of them. Three or four owners on one team is perfectly normal.
  4. If you beat somebody in an earlier coin flip that afternoon and they are in this one too, you sit this one out and they go for it without you. Once they have picked up a team, you are level again and the next one is a normal coin flip.
  5. Only one team is dropped per request. Working down that owner's drop list, the first team they still hold and are allowed to drop is the one that goes. Allowed means three things: still on the roster, past its three-game hold, and the same kind of team as the one coming in. If none of them qualify, the request does not go through, and the owner is told which rule stopped it.

What only you can do

Three actions no other owner has. Two of them are ordinary; the third is not.

Entering a request for somebody

  1. Sign in as yourself.
  2. On the trades page, choose their name from the Submitting for dropdown.
  3. Fill it in exactly as they would have.

The clock applies to you the same as to them: before Monday 4:00pm AZ it goes into the coin flips, after that it is a first-come claim. The record shows both names, and that owner gets an email telling them you submitted for them.

Recording a trade that happened somewhere else

  1. Use Record a trade.
  2. Pick the owner, the team coming in and the team going out.

This is for a trade that was agreed outside the app. Over the phone, by text, or during the first few weeks while the league is still being tried out and you are entering everything yourself. It is an ordinary trade. It costs that owner the usual $3, it appears in the transaction record with your name as the person who entered it, and the owner is emailed.

It is deliberately not the same thing as Correct a roster. Recording a trade is normal. Correcting one is not.

Fixing something that went wrong

  1. Use the Correction button, not the trade form.
  2. Type what you are changing and why.

The fees are yours to set. Putting one trade right can take two or three roster moves, and nobody should be charged two or three times for it. So the fee sits on each line and you can change it. Set a line to $0 when it is part of a correction rather than a new trade. Enter a credit against an owner when they were charged for something that did not happen.

The money and the rosters are corrected separately on purpose. Sometimes a roster is right and only a charge is wrong, and then there is no roster move to attach the fix to.

Any total that has been adjusted is marked as such on the What Each Owner Owes table, with the reason you typed. A figure that does not match the number of trades and does not explain itself reads as a mistake, which is worse than either the wrong number or no number.

This changes who holds what directly, outside the normal windows. It should be rare, because if everything ran properly there is nothing to correct. The affected owners are emailed, it is marked as a correction in the record, and the number of them is shown to you. If that number gets past two or three, tell Casey — it means something is not working.

Settling Monday yourself

There is a switch on the same page with two positions, and it stays there permanently.

  • Automatic — the schedule settles the week a few minutes after 4:00pm AZ every Monday and you do nothing. This is how it runs once the whole league is on it.
  • By hand — the schedule settles nothing and waits for you to press Run the coin flips. This is how the first few weeks run, and it is there afterwards for any week you want to hold back.

The button is there in both positions. Under automatic you would only use it to settle a week early, or to catch somebody who was missed.

If you missed somebody

You run it at 4:05 and then open an email that had been sitting unread since 3:50. It happens.

Use Undo week N, add the request you missed, and run it again.

Undo puts every roster back to how it stood at 4:00pm AZ and keeps every list exactly as it was typed — nobody re-enters anything. Then the whole week is settled from scratch with everything in it.

That is the only way to get the right answer, and it is worth understanding why. A late request does not just add a trade. It can join a coin flip another owner won. That changes who won that team, and that changes who has to stand aside for the next one. Settling the latecomer on top of the earlier result cannot reach the week the league should have had.

Two things to know about undo.

  • Only do it before you have told anybody the results. Running the week again can legitimately give a team to a different owner. The first set of results was wrong rather than unlucky — but somebody who has already been told they won Navy will not see it that way.
  • First come claims made since are undone too, and handed back to you. They were granted against rosters that are about to be settled differently, so they cannot simply be left standing. The confirmation lists them before anything happens, and after you re-run the week each one waits on the Run tab with a Try this one again button. If the team went to somebody else in the new flips, that claim is refused with the reason — which you can pass on, because it was a good claim when they made it.

There is also a plain second run, which settles only what has come in since and cannot change anything already decided. It is right when the person you missed wanted a team nobody else asked for. If you are not sure, undo.

Sending the results yourself

A second switch, next to the first one, decides whether the tool emails owners their results.

  • On — each owner is emailed within a minute of a week being settled.
  • Off — nobody is emailed, and the results appear on screen for you to send instead. Copy every owner’s email gives you all of them in one block, each with the address it goes to, ready to paste.

Off is how the first few weeks run, so nothing reaches the league until you have looked at it. Turning it on is part of stage three.

Sign-in links are not affected by this switch and cannot be. They are how anybody gets into the page at all, including you. Turning results emails off never stops somebody signing in.

If somebody argues about a coin flip

You can re-run it and get the identical result. The flip is worked out from the week, the division and the team rather than being random, so the same week always comes out the same way. Show them twice and the argument is over.

What to do when something changes

Five situations, each one a small edit.

Each of these is a small edit on the Access tab. Nothing has to be rebuilt and nobody else has to be involved. The change takes effect immediately.

An owner gets a new email address

  1. Type the new address over the old one.

That is all. The old address stops working straight away, and their phone stays signed in.

An owner leaves and somebody new takes the team

  1. Type the new owner's address over the old one.
  2. Type an x in the Retire? cell on that row.

Within five minutes the x disappears and Signed in reads 0. That is the confirmation; nothing else needs checking.

Step 2 is the one people forget, and it is the important one. Step 1 stops the old owner getting new links, but it does not reach the phone he is already signed in on. Step 2 does.

The seat keeps its roster, its points and its history. Only the person changes.

An owner leaves and nobody has taken over

  1. Change Status on that row from active to inactive.

The seat still shows up everywhere; it just cannot submit anything. Set it back to active when somebody takes it on.

An owner has no email address

  1. Leave their Email cell blank.
  2. They call or text you, and you enter their trades under their seat. See the next section.

There is deliberately no second way of doing this — a code handed out instead would just be a password, and it would be lost by next August.

An owner says the page has them as the wrong person

  1. Check their row on the Access tab. Their email address is almost certainly on the wrong seat.
  2. Fix it, then type an x in Retire? on both rows involved.

Rolling it out

Three stages. Owners are not let in until it has been run for real for a few weeks.

Nobody is asked to trust this on day one. It goes live in three stages, and the league only notices the third.

Stage one — it runs, and only Bob uses it

The Sheet and the script go live. Nothing changes for the other owners: they keep emailing and phoning Bob exactly as they do now.

What changes is what Bob does with those messages. His Monday looks like this:

  1. Owners email him their requests through the day, as always, up to 4:00pm AZ.
  2. He puts each one into the page under that owner’s name, using Submitting for. These are requests, not finished trades — he is typing in what the owner asked for.
  3. After 4:00pm AZ, once they are all in, he presses Run the coin flips himself.
  4. The page settles everything, including any team more than one owner wanted, and emails each owner what happened.
  5. First come, first served requests arrive through Tuesday and he enters those as they come, the same way.

The important part is that the tool is doing the real work from week one. Bob is doing the typing, not the deciding — the coin flips, the hold rules, the like-for-like check and the running totals are all being run for real. If they are right for three weeks with two people watching, they are right in December with twenty-seven.

Two switches on that page are set for this stage. Settling is by hand, so the schedule waits for him. Results emails are off, so nothing reaches the league until he has looked at it and sent it himself. Both are ordinary settings rather than a temporary mode, and they are flipped the other way at stage three.

Only two people sign in during this stage, which is also the cheapest possible test of the sign-in: if it works for two, the twenty-seventh is not different.

Stage two — a few weeks of watching it

Run it this way for two or three weeks. The buttons have already been tested. What is being checked now is whether the record the page produces matches what actually happened in the league. Compare the Transactions tab against what Bob would have written down. If those agree for three weeks, they will agree in December.

Anything that comes up gets fixed while the audience is two people rather than twenty-seven.

Stage three — the owners

Fill in the remaining email addresses. Set settling to automatic and results emails on. Then tell the league. From that Monday, owners enter their own requests, Monday settles itself a few minutes after four, and everyone is emailed what happened without you touching it.

Bob keeps everything he had. Record a trade and Submitting for do not go away — they are how anybody without email, or having a bad week, still gets their trade in. The job that disappears is being the switchboard, not being the commissioner.
