Reality Football League · 2026 season

League rules

Every way a team earns a point, and every rule the league runs on. Version 2026-09-09c, generated 11 September 2026.

The league

The Reality Football League will consist of three divisions of 9 owners each. A random drawing will be held immediately prior to the league draft to place each Owner in one of the divisions. A separate draft will then be held within each division to fill the owners’ team rosters.

Each owner will draft 8 college (from FBS or FCS schools only) and 3 NFL teams which they will track for the entire season, including playoff games. Each win your team accumulates equals 1 point, each tie is 0.5 point and losses equal zero points. Both college and NFL teams can accumulate points for as many games as they play, including playoffs, bowl games, etc.

Pots and payouts

Each quarter, typically a month’s worth of games, the Owners in each division that scored the most points for that quarter will win quarterly payouts. The singular owner with the most points of all divisions in that quarter will win the 1st place “Quarterly Pot”; the two other Owners from the other two divisions will win the 2nd place and 3rd place “Quarterly Pot” respectively. A small “wild card” pot will be given out each quarter to one runner-up finisher with the highest point total.

Quarterly winners are based on points for wins and ties only. After the Super Bowl game, the four Owners from each division with the most points will share in the “Season Pot”. Season Pot winners are based on wins and ties, plus bonus point accumulation.

The Owner with the most total overall points for the season will be the League Champion and awarded the “Champions Pot”, which will consist of 70% of the money from all trades made from all divisions. A “runner-up pot” will go to the Owner with the 2nd most overall points for the season and will consist of 30% of the money from all trades made from all divisions.

The draft

The draft will be held at 6:00pm on a Friday in late August. We will always have a reservation starting at 5pm, so come early to have a pre-draft beer. Worst case, please be there a few minutes before the 6pm start out of respect for your fellow owners, as we need every owner there to draft (or if an owner can’t be there, they provide a draft list for another owner to draft for them).

There will be 11 draft rounds in each division. Each owner will draft 8 college and 3 NFL teams. Each owner drafts one team per round, which may be either a college or NFL team, as long as the 8 college and 3 NFL team requirement is met by the end of the 11 rounds.

Order of draft selection in each round will be determined as follows: Rounds #1, #3, #5, #7, #9 and #11 will be random — you will pull a number (between 1 and the number of owners) from a hat. The number you draw is your draft position for that round. The round immediately following a “hat” round will follow in reverse order: a #1 pick in the hat round picks last in the following round, the #2 pick picks next to last, and so on. Each drafted team must be held for 3 games. There will be a 2 minute time limit for each Owner in each round.

Quarters

The first, third and fourth quarters are 5 weeks long. The second quarter is 4 weeks long. The fourth quarter includes the remaining regular season college and NFL games and the conference championship games, which are played inside week 18 and are part of the regular season.

Everything after that is postseason and has its own column. Every bowl, every FBS and FCS playoff round and every NFL playoff game is scored in Post, not in a quarter — including bowls played in mid-December, inside league weeks 15 to 18. A postseason game has no quarter to sit in, so whatever a final score settles in it — the win, and the 40+/50+ and shutout halves — is postseason too.

Transactions

Cost: $3 per transaction (or more as decided by Owner vote prior to the draft).

A transaction is considered to be the movement of one team for another. Any team not currently owned in your division is eligible to be picked up. All transaction money goes into the Champions Pot. Once the Trade Commissioner receives the written or verbal transaction, that deal is irrevocable and the Owner must keep their new teams for a minimum of three games played.

Only college teams may be traded for college teams and NFL teams for NFL teams, to sustain the 8 college / 3 NFL requirement.

The team being traded away must have at least 1 regular season game still to be played, while the team being picked up must have at least three games remaining on its regular season. Potential bowl or playoff games do not count toward the minimum game requirements. There is a trade deadline that will occur in early December — you will only be able to trade pro teams at that time, as no college team would have three guaranteed games left.

An owner may not trade away a team after its regular season games are complete.

Any team returned to the pool will be considered inactive for one week, after which that team is re-available to be picked up by any owner via the normal trade guidelines.

How to trade, and the trade window

The college or NFL team trade window for the next week’s games is 9am each Monday until 5pm each Tuesday night (Arizona time).

There is a “peloton” window, whereby all trades received in that window are considered received at the same time — and eligible for a flip-off if two or more owners are seeking the same team. The peloton window is 9am through 4pm on the Monday of each week. Trades received after 4pm Monday until the 5pm Tuesday close of the trade window will be approved on a first-come, first-served basis.

The method to make your trade is to email the commissioner. He will confirm or reject the trade with each owner as soon as possible as it is received; during the peloton window you will find out about your trade by 6pm Monday.

You don’t pay for your trades as they occur — they are kept in an overall ledger and the final amount is announced at the end of the season. To make a trade, notify the commissioner of the team(s) you are dropping and the team(s) you want to pick up. It is often a good idea to identify back-up teams in case you lose a flip for your first choice.

Every way a team earns a point. Each row is paid once per occurrence, and the rows are cumulative — a team that wins the FBS championship is paid for making the field, for each round it won, for reaching the semi-finals, for winning the final, and for being champion. Nothing in the list replaces anything else in it. A regular-season win and a postseason win are the same point: a playoff game is a game.
